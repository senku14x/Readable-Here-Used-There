## 5. Interpretation and next decision

**Gates.** Every orthogonalized direction has |cos| < 1e-4 with ĝ (asserted). The re-run ĝ rows reproduce the pilot's ĝ rows bitwise on all 48 checked arrays (same rendering, same α_t), so cross-run comparison is exact. Realized writes ρ 0.998–1.002, κ ≥ 0.9997 on every condition; carrier damage nil. A first run of this stage saved no RESID_P projections because of a stage-name check in the loader; its arrays are kept as `raw_orthopanel_noresid_run1.npz` and nothing from them is reported.

**Registered bar (max |I_perp| < 0.5 |I_ĝ|).** Fails at L48–50 on J_NP (cov01_perp 0.096 and pc01_perp 0.095 vs ĝ 0.126; 3 of 16 orthogonalized directions ≥ half ĝ, six reciprocal 3/3) and on RESID_P (0.026 vs 0.033). Passes at L51–59 on J_NP (max 0.43, wrong-signed; largest reciprocal 0.35 vs ĝ 1.07), on RESID_P L51–59 (0.32 vs 1.03) and on LOGITS (0.76 vs 1.96). So at the project's default window, directions with no ĝ component reproduce three quarters of the interaction with the same reciprocal sign pattern; at the late window ĝ is 2.5–3× the strongest orthogonal direction, but several orthogonal covariance-matched directions still carry 30 % of it, reciprocal 3/3. Removing the ĝ component from cov01 and pc01 barely changed their effect (0.114 → 0.096; 0.122 → 0.095), so their pilot effect was not carried by their ĝ component. The isotropic directions do nothing at L48–50 and little at L51–59: what carries the interaction is alignment with high-variance directions of the block-35 residual, of which ĝ is one.

**Native-level rows.** Moved to their own opposite-arm level, `cov01` and `pc01` give I of 0.043–0.055 at L48–50 (a third of ĝ) and `cov01` 0.32 at L51–59 (30 %). They are weaker independent handles at their natural dose, not null ones.

**The shared consequence.** Across all 19 directions tested (ĝ, 16 orthogonalized, 2 native), the interaction I is predicted by the direction's effect on the resident readout with no source change, ½(Δm_M − Δm_N) under the move alone: r = 0.97 at L48–50 and 0.999 at L51–59. Whatever a direction does to the readability of the word already present, it does in proportion to the incremental effect of a later source replacement. Algebraically ΔC_M = shift(swap) − shift(noswap), so a direction that scales the readout of the currently sourced word, whichever word it is, produces exactly this reciprocal signature. The temporal-order design excludes the narrow reading that the move only changes already-introduced donor content, as the design says (H1.4 identification note), but it does not separate "task-conditioned change in future source influence" from "a readout gain on the sourced word that applies to resident and incoming content alike", and the data are consistent with the second for every direction, ĝ included.

**Reading under the project's branches.** H1.5: "matched controls reproduce the structured interaction → weaken specificity; investigate shared causal consequences rather than search for flattering controls." The shared consequence is identified above. The licensed statements at pilot scale are: (i) the fitted block-35 coordinate, moved to natural levels, reciprocally changes both the resident readout and the incremental transfer of a post-block-36 source replacement, on three instruments; (ii) at L48–50 this is not specific to ĝ among ĝ-orthogonal high-variance directions at equal dose; (iii) at L51–59 ĝ is the strongest by a factor of about 3 but not unique; (iv) across directions the transfer interaction and the resident-content shift are the same quantity to within measurement.

**Next decision (for discussion, nothing launched).** Scaling to the 16 evaluation pairs would tighten the intervals but would not change (ii)–(iv). The discriminating experiments are: (a) an estimand that a pure readout gain cannot produce, for example the tagged-selection organism (H1.8), where a gain on "the sourced word" would raise all three sources together while selection would raise only the pointed one; (b) the target-presentation and wording factorial (H1.7), to see whether the resident-content shift and the interaction dissociate under any wording; (c) a consumer (H3), since a readout gain and a transfer change make the same lens prediction but need not make the same behavioral one.
