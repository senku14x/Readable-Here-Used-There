# What the answer depends on at the question: the question-turn ladder (H3, Amendments 5–10)

_Consolidated write-up, frozen 2026-09-24 on branch `extension`. The two-hop organism is frozen: no further amendment is registered or run on it. Every number is copied from a committed table, or computed from committed raw arrays by a committed script with no model forwards (§9 says which). The per-stage reports are the evidence of record (§8). Where this write-up corrects wording in them or in its own earlier versions, §9 lists the correction. Registered predictions and post-hoc analyses are kept apart in §7._

## 0. Claims and red-team

**Claims.** The write-up argues for these three and nothing broader. Every share is a share of the question-turn intervention's effect (+11.16 nats), not of the whole clue-swap effect, unless stated.

1. **The selected vocabulary-J directions are privileged, but most of the question-turn effect lies outside their span, and much of their advantage may sit in the scored answer's own directions.** Removing the 25–64 vocabulary directions that best fit the donor's change on the question-turn positions keeps about 0.61 of the effect. Removing isotropic random directions matched in rank and norm keeps 0.95–0.96. The best 25 vocabulary directions install about 0.39, against about 0.13 for 25 fitted functional-Jacobian directions that capture more activation energy. The answer's own two-direction plane alone installs about 0.37, so a broad privilege for non-answer vocabulary directions is not established. The pattern holds on 35 items across six kinds of two-hop fact.
2. **The question-turn state already carries the current answer, and its transfer into a neighbouring question runs largely along the receiving question's answer directions.** The answer's two-direction plane installs 0.37 of the effect against 0.19 for the intermediate's, and more in all 35 items. A capital question's state, written into a language question about the same city, reverses the capital ordering there in 26 of 28 cells. The same transplant pushes the language question toward the donor's language at 0.61 of that question's own change. With the receiving answer's directions projected out, the country plane carries only 0.04 of that push, so Amendment 9's "country dominant" is withdrawn. In the mirror direction 0.15 remains, and a smaller upstream component is plausible there.
3. **With the tested J-derived span at the answer positions held at clean, about 0.41 of the question-turn effect still arrives, about nine tenths of it through the last layer's direct read of the question turn.** The tested intermediate/answer span is held at its clean value at every scored answer position through block 62, with the readouts there verified at −0.04 and −0.03. Then 0.41 [0.38, 0.45] of the effect still arrives, and cutting the last layer's attention from the answer positions to the question-turn positions leaves 0.04. Under the clean-answer-through-block-62 intervention, the surviving late effect is delivered by the last layer's direct read of the eight template tokens at the end of the question turn. Cutting the question-word positions removes no detectable positive effect. A read site is not the semantic identity of what is stored there.

**Red-team.** What a skeptical reader should ask, and where the answer is:

- *Does "outside the selected span" mean "unreadable"?* No. The pursuit selects junk tokens alongside real content (§4.1). Claim 1 concerns the span this dictionary selects, and Claim 3 the span clamped at the answer positions. Neither shows that the remaining state is unreadable to other bases or readers.
- *What are the causal controls for privilege?* Isotropic random directions matched in rank and norm (Stage 2), and Amendment 10's functional-Jacobian dictionary. The covariance-matched N(0, Σ) comparison concerns only activation-energy capture (§4.1, §5).
- *Is the vocabulary advantage a property of vocabulary directions in general?* Not shown. The answer's own two-direction plane installs 0.37 against 0.39 for the best 25 (§4.1).
- *Are the rows the model's natural computation?* No. Every row is a fixed state trajectory: the written component is held at a set value through blocks 36–62. Shares compare trajectories; they are not a partition of one computation (§5). For scale, the question-turn intervention reaches 0.46 of the natural clue-swap effect on the same items (§4.1).
- *Was the clamp at the answer position complete?* For the tested span, from Amendment 9 on. The Stage 2 clamp was partial: readouts at the answer position stayed at about +1.5. Amendment 8 verified the readouts at about zero but clamped only the first answer token, while the endpoint also scores continuation tokens (silly mistake 5). Amendment 9 closes both (§4.4).
- *Is the answer-plane advantage an artefact of the output vocabulary's geometry?* The answer-smuggling screen found no relation in which the answer's unembedding row is an affine image of the intermediate's (low power; §4.2).
- *Does one head carry the last layer's read?* Not shown causally. Head 22 dominates a linearised first-token direct-effect attribution in which positive and negative head contributions cancel, and no head was ablated (§4.4).
- *Are numbers from different runs comparable?* Only where stated. The Amendment 4 numbers in §2 come from 12 released items in bf16; the ladder uses 35 admitted items in float32. Every share in §4 is on the sequence endpoint, except the per-head attribution, which is first-token and labelled so.
- *One model?* Yes: Qwen3.6-27B (hybrid attention), thinking off, one question wording, same-template minimal-pair donors. No claim here extends past that (§5).

## 1. Abstract

The workspace paper argues that representations a Jacobian lens can verbalize form a global workspace that downstream computation draws on. This project asks whether what the lens reads is what the model's answer depends on. In a copy-then-answer organism built on the paper's own two-hop items, earlier stages found that a readable copy of the answer in the copied text moves the answer far less than the source does while the source is visible. This write-up asks the same question at the question turn, where the answer is produced. Writing the donor's question-turn state into the clean run moves the answer by 0.46 of the natural clue-swap effect on the same items. Across 35 items and six relation types, the vocabulary directions a greedy pursuit selects carry about two fifths of that question-turn effect. That is far more than isotropic random directions or Jacobian directions of random output vectors carry, but not most of it, and the answer's own two directions carry almost as much as the best 25. Within the tested directions, the answer's carry about twice what the intermediate's do, and the answer is already strongly carried by the question-turn state. Transplanting the state can also move a neighbouring consumer's answer, but Amendment 10 shows that this transfer is largely aligned with the receiving consumer's own answer directions rather than cleanly isolating a consumer-independent country variable. With the tested J-derived intermediate and answer span held at clean at every scored answer position, about 0.41 of the question-turn effect still arrives. About nine tenths of that surviving part depends on the final layer's direct read of the question turn, which Amendment 10 locates at the template tokens that end the turn. The results are compatible with a causally important verbalizable interface, but not with the tested vocabulary span being the dominant causal bottleneck for this intervention.

## 2. What was known, and what this adds

The workspace paper's causal case studies swap the lens coordinates of an intermediate (for example Spain ↔ Canada) and show the answer moves. Amendment 4 ran that swap on this organism, on the 12 released items in bf16. On the sequence endpoint, the all-position intermediate-coordinate swap moves the answer by about 0.41 [0.30, 0.53] of the full-residual reference, while the corresponding answer-word swap moves it by about 0.54 [0.41, 0.67]. Confined to the question turn, the two swaps give 0.20 and 0.43, so the question turn carries about half of the intermediate swap's effect. The answer-word swap is not larger at every position set. At the clue span the intermediate swap gives 0.09 against 0.06, and at the copied carrier 0.07 against 0.06. On the first-token endpoint the all-position values are 0.46 and 0.63. An earlier version of this section paired the first-token 0.63 with the sequence 0.41 (§9).

Earlier H3 stages established a position asymmetry. Donor content installed in the copied text shifts the answer by 0.02–0.27 of what the same content does at the source, while the source is visible (`STATUS.md`). The carrier swap installs the swapped intermediate at the carrier readout as strongly as the whole swap does, yet moves the answer by only 0.07. Where the intermediate is readable and where the answer has leverage come apart. This write-up moves from *where* a lens-readable copy sits to *what* carries the answer at a position that matters, the question turn, and asks how much of that the tested lens directions account for.

## 3. Setup

- **Organism.** A user turn gives a clue ("the capital of the country where Barcelona is located"), asks the model to copy a sentence, and a final question turn asks for the one-word answer. The question turn's token ids are identical across all prompts. The *donor* prompt differs only in the clue's cue, for most items one city name (Toronto), so its answer differs (Ottawa, not Madrid).
- **Intervention.** At every block 36–62, the question-turn positions except the last (`q_pre`, 27 positions) are overwritten with a fixed target built from the donor's state. The final position s, where the answer is produced, is never written except by the clamps of §4.4. The residual is carried in float32 from block 35 so writes are exact (ρ = κ = 1.000 throughout).
- **Endpoint.** The sequence log-prob margin log P(donor's answer) − log P(own answer), summed over tokens and log-sum-exp'd over spellings, minus the clean run's margin. A row's **share** is its item-mean margin over the full question-turn donor's (`q_full_pre`, +11.16 nats [10.53, 11.80] on the admitted items, 35/35 positive). Intervals on margins are item-clustered t intervals; share intervals are cluster bootstraps over items (2 000 resamples). The **natural clue-swap effect** is the margin of the natural donor rendering (the recipient prompt with the donor's clue) on the same items and run: +24.27 nats [22.98, 25.56]. It is the same-item denominator for scale (§4.1).
- **Lens directions.** For a vocabulary word v at block l, the folded direction J_lᵀ(γ ⊙ w_v) is the direction whose coordinate the Jacobian lens (J_NP) reads as v. A *plane* is the span of two such directions (for example Spain and Canada). The *J_k part* of the donor's change is its least-squares fit on the k directions chosen greedily from all 248 320 vocabulary words; the *complement* is what is left. The **tested J-derived span** at the answer positions (Amendments 8–10) is every single-token form of the item's four words (intermediate, swap-to, answer, swap-answer), their aliases and translations, plus the 25 vocabulary directions that best fit the donor's change at s (rank median 58).
- **Items.** Twelve released two-hop items (Stage 1) and 30 constructed ones over five templates; 35 of 42 pass admission. An item is admitted if the model is competent in all four copy sentences and swapping the intermediate at mid depth moves the answer more than swapping the answer. Every stage was registered before its forwards (Amendments 5–10 of `H3/design_specs/two_hop_organism.md`).

### 4.1 Most of the question-turn effect lies outside the selected vocabulary-J span

Writing the donor's question-turn state into the clean run moves the answer by +11.16 nats (35/35 items; 50 of 140 cells cross). On the same 35 items and in the same run, the natural donor rendering moves it by +24.27 nats. The question-turn intervention therefore reaches 0.46 [0.44, 0.48] of the natural clue-swap effect (ratio of item means; per item 0.32–0.58). The shares below are shares of the question-turn effect, not of the natural clue-swap effect.

Removing the donor change's component on the best k vocabulary directions keeps 0.73, 0.64, 0.61 and 0.61 of the question-turn effect at k = 2, 8, 25 and 64. Removing k isotropic random directions matched in rank and norm keeps 0.98, 0.97, 0.96 and 0.95 (Figure 1a; Stage 2, every row positive in 35/35 items). Removing the best 25 costs 0.39 of the effect, where removing 25 random directions costs 0.04, and the best 25 installed alone carry 0.39. The curve flattens after k = 25, while the fitted part saturates at 0.27–0.29 of the change's norm at layers 51–59. The selected directions are strongly privileged against isotropic random directions, and they are not most of the effect.

The advantage is not generic to arbitrary output-Jacobian directions. The best 25 vocabulary directions install about 0.39 of the question-turn effect, versus about 0.13 [0.11, 0.14] for 25 fitted random functional-Jacobian directions (f_u = J_lᵀ(γ ⊙ u) with u drawn from N(0, Cov W_U), selected by the same pursuit; Amendment 10 Part C). The functional directions capture more activation energy than the vocabulary directions (0.084 against 0.054 of the change's energy on the question-turn positions). Removing them keeps 0.83, against 0.61 for the best 25 vocabulary directions. However, the answer's own two-direction vocabulary plane alone installs about 0.37 (§4.2). The experiment therefore does not establish a broad privilege for non-answer vocabulary directions; much of the observed vocabulary advantage may be concentrated in directions closely tied to the scored answer.

The dictionary itself limits how far "outside the selected span" can be pushed. The two-direction pursuit contains the exact intermediate or swap-to word in only 4 % of its fits on the question-turn positions at layers 51–59, and junk tokens (`\n\n`, end-of-text, digits) dominate the pooled selection. At k = 64 the fitted part captures no more of the change's energy than 64 directions drawn from N(0, Σ) of the model's own activations (0.066 against 0.069). That comparison concerns activation-energy capture only, not causal privilege: by k = 64 the selected span no longer captures unusually large state energy. At the two informative positions (the last question token and the answer position), 7.5–14 % of the chosen directions are the item's words, their translations or their close associates (up to 22 % by weight), against 1 % pooled over all question-turn positions (a post-hoc check, §7). The chosen directions carry the entity at their head and pad the fit with noise in their tail. Claim 1 is therefore about the span this dictionary selects; a better basis could name more.

Stage 2 reproduces the twelve released items of Stage 1 to the second decimal, and its shares on all 42 items differ from those on the 35 admitted items by at most 0.028; constructed and released donors behave alike.

### 4.2 Within the tested lens directions, the leverage belongs more to the answer than to the intermediate

Installing only the intermediate's plane (for Barcelona–Toronto, the Spain and Canada directions) moves the answer 0.19 of the way. Installing only the answer's plane (Madrid and Ottawa) moves it 0.37: more in all 35 items (+2.08 nats [+1.63, +2.54]) and in every relation type, at slightly smaller norm (Figure 1b). Removing the intermediate plane costs 0.21 of the effect, the answer plane 0.38, and both together 0.43. About three quarters of the intermediate plane's effect (overlap 0.72 [0.64, 0.79]) is already carried by the answer plane, and the intermediate plane adds 0.057 on its own (Amendment 8 (a)). The two planes' directions have a mean cosine of 0.44, so part of the overlap is geometric. The intermediate's plane still has a nonzero within-question causal effect (0.19).

A skeptic might suspect the vocabulary geometry. If the answer's output direction were a fixed linear image of the intermediate's, the two planes would be the same handle in different coordinates. The answer-smuggling screen fitted that map per relation and found no relation where it predicts held-out pairs better than chance: residual fractions were 0.94–1.00 against mismatched-pair nulls of 1.00–1.04. The test has little power at 10–22 pairs per relation.

### 4.3 The question-turn state carries the current answer and transfers strongly into another consumer's answer geometry

The strongest test of "the answer is already computed at the question turn" moves the state to a question that asks for something else (Amendment 8 (c), seven city pairs). The change in the question-turn state between "the capital of the country where Lyon is" and "…where Naples is" is written into "the language spoken in the country where Lyon is". Across the seven pairs, the written state pushes the language question toward the donor's capital (Rome over Paris in the example) by 0.84 of the capital question's own push. It reverses the capital ordering in 26 of 28 cells, although the question asks for a language. The mirror (the language question's change written into the capital question) pushes toward the donor's language by 0.96 of the language question's own push. Each question's state carries its own answer, strongly enough that a different question's output registers it.

The same capital state also moves the language question toward the donor's language (Italian in the example) by 0.61 [0.54, 0.69] of that question's own change, in 7 of 7 pairs. The mirror moves the capital question toward the donor's capital by 0.45 [0.38, 0.50]. Random vectors of the same size move either by less than 0.02. Amendment 8 registered this as "upstream content present". Amendments 9 and 10 show that this reading does not isolate a consumer-independent upstream variable.

**The transfer runs largely along the receiving question's answer directions** (Figure 1d). Amendment 9 split the language-direction transfer by readable plane. The country plane (France, Italy) alone gave 0.80 of the transfer push, the city plane (Lyon, Naples) 0.28 and a random plane 0.005, and Amendment 9 registered the reading "country dominant", with the overlap between country and answer directions as a caveat. Amendment 10 tested that overlap. In the language question, the receiving answer's own plane (French, Italian) alone gives 0.81 [0.78, 0.83], the same as the country plane (0.80 [0.77, 0.82]). With the receiving answer's directions projected out, the country plane gives 0.04 [0.03, 0.06], and a matched random plane gives 0.003. The country and language planes share principal cosines of 0.86 and 0.59 at layers 51–59. In the mirror, the capital plane alone gives 0.52 [0.39, 0.62] and the country plane 0.63 [0.54, 0.70], and 0.15 [0.13, 0.18] of country-specific push survives the projection. "Country dominant" is withdrawn. Most of the apparent country transfer in the language direction is explained by overlap with the receiving answer geometry. A smaller upstream component remains plausible in the mirror direction.

### 4.4 A late route outside the tested J-derived span at the answer positions

The consumer clamps hold a span of lens directions at the answer position at its clean values through blocks 36–62. Meanwhile the question-turn positions carry the donor's change with its best 25 vocabulary directions removed (0.61 of the effect, Figure 1c). From Amendment 8 on, the clamped span is the tested J-derived span of §3. The ladder of clamps tightened over three amendments:

| clamp at the answer position | readouts there (intermediate / answer) | share of the question-turn effect kept | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| none (best 25 removed on the question-turn positions) | +2.67 / +2.45 | 0.61 [0.57, 0.65] | Stage 2 |
| the 25 atoms that best fit the donor's change there, first answer token only | +1.49 / +1.32 | 0.51 [0.47, 0.55] | Stage 2 |
| verified: the tested J-derived span, first answer token only | −0.04 / −0.03 | 0.43 [0.40, 0.48] | Amendment 8 |
| verified: the tested J-derived span, every scored answer position | −0.04 / −0.03 | **0.41 [0.38, 0.45]** | Amendment 9 |
| random frame of the same rank and norm, every scored answer position | +2.65 / +2.43 | 0.61 [0.57, 0.64] | Amendment 9 |

The Stage 2 clamp was partial. Amendment 8's was complete at the first answer token, but not at the continuation tokens of multi-token spellings, which the endpoint also scores (silly mistake 5). Clamping every scored position removed 0.02 more, all of it from continuation tokens. About 0.41 of the question-turn intervention effect survives while the tested J-derived intermediate/answer span at every scored answer position is held at its clean value through block 62. The donor-induced change in the tested J-derived span is suppressed at the answer positions through block 62; the experiment does not establish that the surviving state is unreadable to all alternative bases or readers.

Amendment 9 then cut routes at the final block, which is a full-attention layer:

- With the last layer's attention from the answer positions to all question-turn positions (`q_pre`) cut, the clamped complement keeps **0.04** [0.03, 0.05]. The post-softmax weight is exactly 0.
- With every answer position held entirely at its clean state through block 62, so that only the last layer's read can carry anything, it keeps **0.33** [0.30, 0.37].
- With both cuts it keeps exactly 0 in every item.

Cutting that one read removes about nine tenths of the surviving effect overall, and at least three quarters in every relation type. For the whole question-turn change, the last layer's read alone delivers 0.41 [0.37, 0.46] of the effect, and cutting it leaves 0.69, so the two routes are partly redundant. For scale, this last-layer-only row is +4.59 nats [4.01, 5.17] on the 35 admitted items. That is 0.19 [0.17, 0.21] of the natural clue-swap effect on the same items (ratio of item means, same run and regime). The earlier cross-run ratio, 4.59 over the 12-item full-residual reference of +23.88, gives the same 0.19. Throughout, 0.41 is a share of the question-turn full-donor effect.

What travels this route lies outside the best 25 vocabulary directions on the question-turn positions. Its effect does not pass through the tested J-derived span at the answer positions before the last layer. Nothing here shows that it is unreadable to other bases, and the last layer writes the answer, which the lens reads at the output.

**Where the last layer reads (Amendment 10 Part D).** Part D worked under the clean-answer-through-block-62 intervention, which holds the answer positions entirely at clean through block 62 and delivers 0.411 of the question-turn effect. Under that intervention, the surviving late effect is delivered by the last layer's direct read of the eight template tokens at the end of the question turn: `<|im_end|>`, newline, `<|im_start|>`, `assistant`, newline, `<think>`, blank line and `</think>`. Cutting the last layer's read of those tokens removes 1.00 [0.996, 1.002] of it. Cutting the actual question-word positions removes no detectable positive effect (−0.019 [−0.025, −0.013]), and cutting the pre-question tokens removes −0.038 [−0.044, −0.032]; both cuts slightly raise the delivered effect. A read site is not the semantic identity of what is stored there. These data show where the last layer reads the donor-induced change, not that the template tokens contain the answer.

**Per-head attribution.** Each block-63 head's output change at the answer position under that intervention was pushed onto the first-token margin through the final norm, linearised at the clean state; block 63's MLP is not in this path. In the linearised first-token direct-effect attribution, head 22 contributes about +3.08 nats out of roughly +4.25 nats of total positive head contribution (~72 %). Head 21 contributes about −0.76 nats. Head 22 is ~0.98 of the net summed attribution (+3.14) only because positive and negative head contributions cancel. Head 22 puts 0.59 of its attention on the template tokens and 0.02 on the question words. This is a linearised, first-token, direct-effect attribution; it is not a causal head-ablation result.

**Delivery depth (Part E).** The answer positions were held entirely at clean through block B and left free afterwards. For B = 50, 54, 58 and 62 this leaves 0.92, 0.89, 0.82 and 0.41 of the question-turn effect. Under this family of interventions, most of the effect can still be delivered when the answer positions are released only after block 58. The curve compares intervention trajectories; it does not time the natural computation.

**The small route before the last layer (Part A).** With the last layer's read cut and no clamp, the complement row (best 25 removed) keeps 0.24 [0.21, 0.28] of the question-turn effect through the answer positions' own states. Clamping the tested J-derived span reduces it to 0.04 of the question-turn effect. A rank-matched span fitted from a non-lens dictionary of Gaussian directions with the activations' covariance reduces it to 0.06, while capturing about twice the tested span's energy of the donor change at s (0.41 against 0.21). An energy-matched non-lens span reduces it to 0.13 (secondary). The registered reading is "partial". The tested span blocks most of this small route, but a non-lens span of the same rank blocks nearly as much, so the route is not shown to be specific to the lens span. The masked, clamped rows carry a precision caveat of about ±0.01 of share (§5).

## 5. Limitations and threats to validity

- **Fixed trajectories, not natural computation.** Every row holds its written component at a set value through blocks 36–62, so rows are trajectories to compare, not parts of one computation. The additivity residuals of the plane and J_25 splits average under 0.25 nats (intervals within ±0.55), so the shares read as rough components, but they remain components of interventions. The same-item scale ratios compare interventions with the natural clue-swap effect. The 0.46 for the question-turn intervention and the 0.19 for the last-layer-only row are not the fractions of the natural computation that run through the question turn or the last layer.
- **"Readable" means one tested basis and one reader.** The dictionary is the J_NP folded directions of single vocabulary tokens, selected greedily with least-squares refits. It is noisy (junk atoms, other-script forms counted by a mechanical rule), and a basis fitted differently could name more of the change. The causal privilege comparisons are against isotropic rank/norm-matched random directions and Amendment 10's functional-Jacobian dictionary. The covariance-matched N(0, Sigma) comparison concerns only activation-energy capture; it shows that by k = 64 the selected vocabulary span no longer captures unusually large state energy. None of these comparisons bounds how much a better basis or a different reader would find.
- **The vocabulary advantage may be answer-specific.** The answer's own two-direction plane installs 0.37 against 0.39 for the best 25 vocabulary directions. Whether non-answer vocabulary directions are privileged over functional-Jacobian directions was not tested.
- **Attribution and read site are not mechanism.** The head-22 result is a linearised first-token direct-effect attribution with cancelling contributions, and no head was ablated. The template-token result locates where the last layer reads under one intervention; it does not identify what is stored at those tokens.
- **Endpoints and item sets.** The ladder uses the sequence endpoint on 35 admitted items in float32. The Amendment 4 numbers of §2 come from 12 released items in bf16. On the 12 released items shared by both runs, the natural donor rendering reproduces the full-residual reference to −0.002 nats [−0.05, +0.05]; Stage 2 scored no full-residual reference on the constructed items.
- **One model, one setting.** Qwen3.6-27B (hybrid linear and full attention), thinking off, one question wording, four copy sentences, same-template minimal-pair donors. The dense-model replication used elsewhere in the project was not run for this ladder.
- **The hybrid architecture limits cuts.** The last layer is full attention, so its read of the question-turn positions is cut exactly. Earlier layers include linear-attention (recurrent) channels, so no route through the middle of the network is cut completely; the full-state clamp of the answer positions is the construction that avoids needing one.
- **Scale of inference.** 35 items (140 cells) for the ladder, seven pairs (28 cells) for the transfer rows. Shares are ratios of item means with cluster-bootstrap intervals. Per-relation numbers rest on 1–11 items and are descriptive.
- **Precision of the masked, clamped rows.** Half-precision storage of the clamp basis moved one such row by 0.12 nats in the Amendment 10 smoke test, so the Part A rows carry about ±0.01 of share.
- **Two silly mistakes, both caught by construction checks and recorded.** Silly mistake 4: the registered current-base row of Amendment 6 added the accumulated block change at every block (6.5–9× overshoot); its numbers were never read, and the repaired form ran in Stage 2. Silly mistake 5: the consumer clamps of Amendments 6–8 covered only the first answer token while the endpoint also scores continuation tokens; Amendment 9 measured its size (§4.4).
- **Scope of the transfer test.** It separates answer content from other content only through readable planes. With the receiving answer's plane projected out, 0.04 of country-specific push survives in the language direction and 0.15 in the mirror. The cue city was not separated from the country beyond their readable planes.

## 6. What this does and does not say about the workspace claim

The paper's causal evidence is that swapping an intermediate's lens coordinates moves the answer. That holds here (§2), and the selected vocabulary-J directions are privileged against isotropic random directions and against functional-Jacobian directions (§4.1). The results are compatible with a causally important verbalizable interface, but they do not support treating the tested J-vocabulary representation as the dominant causal bottleneck for the question-turn intervention. At this late cut, the strongest readable leverage is already answer-aligned, while a substantial intervention effect remains outside the particular vocabulary span tested here. This mirrors the project's position result: the readable copy in the copied sentence is consulted only weakly while the source is visible.

**Not licensed by this ladder:**

- "The answer bypasses the workspace" or "a route no lens can read." The route's effect lies outside one dictionary's selected span on the question-turn positions and outside the tested span at the answer positions. Other bases were not exhausted, and the last layer writes the answer, which the lens reads at the output.
- "The model ignores the intermediate." This is not licensed: its two-direction plane has a nonzero within-question causal effect (~0.19). The cross-question experiment, however, no longer cleanly identifies a reusable intermediate, because most of the apparent country transfer in the language direction is explained by overlap with the receiving answer geometry.
- "Country dominant." Amendment 10 withdrew it: the transfer runs along the receiving question's answer directions.
- "About 41 % of the computation is non-verbal." The 0.41 is a share of one intervention's effect under one clamp of one tested span.

**Numerical corrections**

1. **Endpoint mix in §2.** "0.41 of the full-residual ceiling … the answer word moves it more (0.63)" paired a sequence-endpoint share with a first-token one. On the sequence endpoint the answer-word swap is 0.54 [0.41, 0.67]; on the first-token endpoint the pair is 0.46 and 0.63. Stage 2 scored the natural donor rendering in every cell, in the same float32 regime and on the same endpoint: +24.27 nats [22.98, 25.56] on the 35 admitted items, reproduced by `probe4`'s own donor rendering to 5 × 10⁻⁷ nats.
